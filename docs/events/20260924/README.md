## Notes

SoTL started with `[Hutchings and Shulman, 1999]` to, I quote:

- foster significant, long-lasting learning for all students
- advance the practice and profession of teaching
- bring to teaching the recognition afforded to other forms of scholarly work

Features of SoTL teaching, also from `[Hutchings and Shulman, 1999]`:

- Be an informed teacher e.g. certain practices of classroom assessment
  and evidence gathering, informed by current ideas about teaching,
  when it invites peer collaboration and review
- being public (“community property”)
- being open to critique and evaluation
- being open in a form that others can build on
- it involves question-asking, inquiry,
  and investigation, particularly around issues of student learning

SoTL second classic is `[Felten, 2013]`,
which that that SoTL follows there principles:

- inquiry into student learning, i.e. try to understand how students learn
- grounded in context, i.e. embrace that teaching differs across many
  factors such as classroom size, field, etc.
- methodologically sound, i.e. use the right methods to try to understand
  how students learn
- conducted in partnership with students, i.e. partner with the students
  to find out how they learn
- appropriately public, i.e. try to understand have students learn in
  such a way that it can be criticized and used by colleagues

such as McKinney (2007), that I cannot read. I've sent UU UB an email.

Criticism of SoTL comes from `[Canning and Masika, 2022]`
(which claim that SoTL started with `[Boyer, 1990]`)
that states that SoTL once was good, later evolved into SoTL 2.0 (see
their table below) and now needs to be thrown out of the window.

<!-- markdownlint-disable MD013 --><!-- Tables cannot be split up over lines, hence will break 80 characters per line -->

.               |SoTL 1.0                                                    |SoTL 2.0
----------------|------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------
Central concerns|A concern for better understandings of teaching and learning|A concern for being and valuing learning and teaching
Arena           |Disseminated and peer reviewed outside an institution.      |An institutional / internal focus.
Definition      |Clearly defined and delineated                              |Inclusive of all that relates to teaching and learning.
Philosophy      |Philosophically inclusive                                   |Philosophically untheorised.
Dissemination   |Externally focused and disseminated.                        |Internally focused and sometimes disseminated.
Leadership      |Research-led                                                |Management-led
Innovation      |Innovates and contributes original knowledge.               |May innovate, but does not disseminate.
Foundation      |Builds on previous scholarship                              |Builds on the needs of the institution or individuals.

Another criticism comes from `[Macfarlane, 2011]`, which states that
having 'teaching professors' implies that there are 'real'
'research professors' and argues for integrating the two and equally
scrutinizing SoTL and other research.
